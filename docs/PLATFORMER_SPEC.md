# Atom Maze platformer (story mode)

Replaces the Atom Maze's room-by-room battle with a side-scrolling platformer: the learner runs and jumps through a level, touches an enemy to start a question battle, and reaches the door. This is the first prototype, played from Games > Atom Maze > "Reactor Trail" (`app/explorer/platformer.tsx`). The old maze (`app/explorer/atom-maze.tsx`, `components/atom-maze/`, `services/atom-maze-progression.ts`'s maze use) stays until this is accepted, then is retired.

Art and sound: Kenney's New Platformer Pack (CC0, `tools/platformer-art/License.txt`; crediting Kenney is welcome, not required). `assets/platformer` stays as the pack's library (its `Sprites` 64 px PNGs, `Vector` SVG sources, `Spritesheets`, `Preview` files and `Sounds`); only what the game USES is moved into `assets/images/platformer/<group>/` (`backgrounds`, `enemies`, `tiles`, `props`, `hud`, `characters`), as double-size pictures required by hand in `components/platformer/sprites.ts`. To use another picture from the pack, add its `require` there in the right group folder and run `node scripts/bake-platformer-sprites-2x.mjs`. Five of the sounds were converted to the SFX catalog (§7).

## 1b. The hero: Shrodi

The hero is Shrodi, the fox mascot, as ONE sprite sheet of 62 frames (8 columns), drawn on the Kenney characters' 128 x 128 canvas (their size and ground line) and baked at twice that, 256 px a frame, so he stays sharp on a 3x phone and when a battle zooms in. His BODY is the Kenney character's own: the arms, legs and torso are copied from the poses `character_yellow_idle`, `walk_a`, `walk_b`, `jump`, `hit`, `front` and `duck` (SVG sources in `tools/platformer-art/Vector/Characters`), with the helmet and face removed and the colours changed: the torso is painted as a white lab coat (fur collar, lapels, pockets, buttons) and stretched 10 px taller (the head and coat's top go up, the feet stay on the ground: `TALLER` in the script; the arms stay at the coat's sides, not up at the neck: `ARMS_RAISE`), with no sleeves: the arms and hands are Shrodi's orange fur, as are the legs, which are drawn close together (`LEGS_TOGETHER`). Arms and legs are drawn behind the coat. Shrodi's real head from `components/mascot/shrodi-art.ts` (ears, glasses, face: it blinks, glances and talks like the mascot) sits where the helmet was, scaled to 0.46 with its chin on the helmet's bottom edge, and his tail swings behind, on the left of the picture (the pictures face right, so the tail trails behind a hero running right; the game flips the picture for left, putting it on the right). The stroke is Kenney's #353541, 4 px at 128 px, as ONE outline round the whole figure. The clips are idle (24 frames: breathing, an ear flick, a glance and a blink), fidget (20: looks up, tilts and lifts a paw), walk (4: step, pass, step with the other foot, pass, from Kenney's own two walking poses plus the idle pose, with the body bobbing and the head swaying against the step), rise, apex, fall, land (2: the duck pose, then a spring back), hit (2) and cheer (6). The table is `components/platformer/hero-anim-core.ts`, shared by the bake script and the game, with `heroFrameIndex` choosing a frame from the hero's state: hurt first, then the air (by vertical speed), the landing squash, the walk (one cycle per `STRIDE` tiles walked, so the feet match the ground and the cycle is the same left or right), and standing, which plays idle twice, then the fidget, and round again. `platformer-core` keeps `distance`, `idleT`, `landT` and `hurtT` on the hero for this. `fillFrame` writes the frame number and `PlatformerWorld`'s `Hero` moves the sheet behind a one-cell window (no React render); `HeroSprite.tsx` does the same for the battle and result cards (`HeroClip`: idle, cheer, hit). Baked by `node scripts/bake-shrodi-hero.mjs`, which writes the editable `tools/platformer-art/Vector/Characters/shrodi_sheet.svg` (the stroke is a filter there) and `assets/images/platformer/characters/character_shrodi_sheet.png` (`--preview <dir>` writes it on the sky colour at 2x). To change a clip, edit `FRAMES` in the script and run it again (a changed frame count is changed in `hero-anim-core.ts`; `npm run test:platformer` checks the sheet's size).

## 2. The level

Rows of text, one character per tile (legend in `platformer-core.ts`): `#` ground, `~` water, `%` lava, `o` coin, `$` gem (five coins), `^` spikes, `Z` saw, `> <` conveyor belts, `_` bridge of logs, `G` gem block, `*` bush, `P` start, `F` door, `C` checkpoint (a flag), `s l n m b B` enemies. Enemies, the start, checkpoints and the door are written in the row ABOVE the ground they stand on. The hero jumps 2.9 tiles high and about 4.8 far, so keep ground steps at 2 or fewer and gaps at 4 or fewer; `test:platformer` checks it and walks the level with a bot.

## 3. Rules

- **Move.** 6 tiles/s, quick acceleration, a short coyote time (0.1 s) and jump buffer (0.12 s). Holding jump goes the full 2.9 tiles; letting go while rising makes a hop of about 0.7.
- **Hearts.** Three. Spikes, a wrong answer or falling in the water cost one; a heart lost gives 1.6 s of blinking grace (`hero.blink`). A won fight gives 0.8 s of grace too, but the hero does not blink: a flicker after a right answer looked like a hurt. A fall returns the hero to the last checkpoint passed.
- **Enemies.** Walkers turn at an edge or a wall; the bee flies side to side; the boss paces a short stretch. Touching one opens a battle. Each has `hp` (correct answers to beat it) and a `tier` (0 easy, 1 medium, 2 hard questions).
- **Battle.** A right answer hits the enemy; at 0 life it is beaten and the hero hops on. A wrong answer costs a heart, ends the battle, pushes the hero away, and the enemy keeps its damage. Answers are graded on the tap. A wrong one shows the explanation and waits for Continue; a right one moves on by itself.
- **The battle is on the map, not a screen of its own.** Touching an enemy freezes the world, and the whole picture zooms in on the pair (340 ms, ease-out; `zoomPlan` scales about the middle so the two land above the question panel, at most 2.2x, the camera stays put) while the panel rises (`BattlePanel`: enemy name and life, the question, TWO options, the verdict). The two face each other and the hero's idle loop plays while the question waits. A multiple choice question keeps its right answer and one wrong one (a true or false one already has two). The answer is graded on the tap (the option pops or shakes), and the fight starts on the same clock (`battle-fx-core.ts`, in the lessons' motion language: anticipation, lunge, contact with a hang, squash, recoil, settle):
  - **Right answer, an attack.** The hero draws back (0..110 ms), leaps at the enemy and hits it at 230 ms; the enemy squashes and is thrown away from the hero (it never flashes: only the hero does, when hurt), then returns (a beaten one stays away and poofs); the hero cheers. A yellow burst and impact lines fly out of the contact point, the picture gets a small zoom punch and a light shake. Life drops at contact; if the enemy still stands the next question settles in, otherwise the view zooms out (260 ms).
  - **Wrong answer, a hurt.** The enemy draws back and lunges at the hero, who flashes red, squashes and is thrown back, then flinches; the picture shakes hard, a red wash crosses the screen, and a heart breaks off the hero and floats up. The heart is lost at contact (hearts at the top, the hurt sound), the reason is shown with Continue, and the view zooms out when it is tapped. The last heart ends the run.
  - Reduced motion: the view cuts in and out, the fight's moves and bursts are not drawn, the answer's colours and the panel still tell what happened.
- **Questions.** Myth Buster bank (`getAllMythBusterQuizQuestions`): multiple choice and true or false only. Never the same question twice in a run (the Atom Maze progression), picked by the enemy's tier, nearest tier when one runs dry. When the whole bank has been used an enemy simply gives way.
- **Goal door.** `F` in a level file is the door (two tiles tall: an arch over a body, `door_*` props). It shows a lock while the boss lives; touching it says so. The core's `goal` event ends the run (the core and the screen still call the goal "flag" in identifiers).
- **Score.** Coin 10, enemy 100, and on reaching the door 200 plus 50 per heart left. **Stars** come from the points: the door gives 1, 60% of the level's best score (`maxScoreOf`: all coins, all enemies, all hearts, the door) gives 2, 85% gives 3 (`starsFor`, `STAR_SHARE`), so running past enemies cannot reach three. **Timer:** seconds played outside battles (`stats.time`, shown under the pause icon), kept as the level's best time; it does not change the stars. Saved with `saveGameScore("platformer", score)`; `recordActivity("game_session", { id: "platformer" })`.

## 4. Controls

Left and right on the left, jump on the right (the deck under the world, so thumbs never cover the action). Each is a `Pan` gesture with `minDistance(0)`, so touches are independent. Back or the header arrow asks "Leave this level?" while playing.

## 5. Rendering

The core is stepped at a fixed 1/60 s from a `requestAnimationFrame` clock (an accumulator, so a slow frame never changes the physics). Each frame `fillFrame` writes the camera, hero and enemy positions in pixels into one array shared value; every moving picture reads its slice in `useAnimatedStyle`, so React never re-renders per frame. The hero's frame is one number into a sprite sheet (see §1b); enemies are two stacked images whose opacity is switched. In a battle the same array carries the fight (squash, flash and offsets per fighter) and the zoom is a transform on one wrapper driven by shared values (`zoom`, a plan and the fight's clock), so a battle costs no React render per frame either. The ground is built in chunks of 8 columns and only the chunks near the view exist. React state changes only on events (coin, hurt, battle, chunk boundary). The camera eases toward a point a little ahead of the hero, in whole pixels so the tiles never shimmer. Reduced motion has no effect on the platformer's physics; the camera easing and the poof are short.

Layout: the world fills the whole screen, top to bottom, with no header. A game HUD floats over it below the status bar (`GameHud.tsx`): the top row is the lessons' own progress bar (same track, fill and shine, filled by how far the hero has run) with a bare PAUSE icon on its right (filled white, no container; it opens `PauseDialog`: Resume, Restart, Leave; Android back opens it too); only the checkpoint flags and the goal door (faded while the boss lives) sit on the bar; under the row, top left, are the hearts with the pack's coin and a compact `x 12` in its digit sprites right below, each popping when it changes. The bar steps aside during a battle and the whole HUD is hidden while the level loads and ends. **Boss introduction.** The first time the hero stands on the ground with the boss 7 tiles ahead (just off screen) the world freezes and a cinematic plays (`boss-intro-core.ts`, one clock in ms): the letterbox bars slide in and the HUD and controls leave (0 to 350); the camera scrolls to the boss (300 to 1200) and zooms in on it (950 to 1450, the same zoom layer as a battle); the boss turns to face the hero and ROARS (1600: it crouches, leaps stretched, slams down squashed, the picture shakes and a ring of stars bursts, with a hit of sound and a vibration) while its name is stamped on the bottom bar with "Guardian of the flag" (from 1700); then the zoom lets go and the camera scrolls back to the hero (3300 to 4200) and the bars leave, and play goes on from the hero's own view (total 4.3 s). It always plays through (no skip). It plays once per run, pauses with the pause dialog, and is skipped with reduced motion. The hero stands in its idle loop while it watches. The backdrop is a stack, from the top: solid sky (the view's colour), the clouds, then a biome picture (hills, trees, desert or mushrooms: its `color` picture ends in the biome's ground colour, its `fade` one fades back into sky) whose bottom edge sits on the ground line so the hills show above the ground tiles, and below a `color` biome a solid fill of its ground (grass, dirt or sand) to the bottom edge (`backdropStack` in `platformer-view.ts`, `BIOMES` in `sprites.ts`, `Backdrop` in `PlatformerWorld.tsx`; each layer slides at its own slow parallax). The cloud picture ends in solid white and the biome picture starts with it, so the biome is drawn over the clouds' last 12% to keep the white band short without covering a bump or a tall tree. It is eight tiles wide in view (`playLayout` in `platformer-view.ts`: tile = min(width / 8, what fits above the controls)); the level's last row sits just above the controls' strip (lifted a little, `GROUND_LIFT`), sky fills the screen above the level, and the ground goes on below it as dirt (`underArt`, `underRows`) to the bottom edge, fading to black behind the touch controls (`BLACK` in the screen, EX-16 in `THEME_EXCEPTION_REGISTER.md`). The controls sit over that fade.

**Emotes** (`emote-core.ts`, `Emote` in `PlatformerWorld.tsx`, pictures `EMOTE_SPRITES`): speech balloons from Kenney's Emotes (CC0; the vector sheet and license are kept in `assets/images/platformer/emotes/source`, the 14 used are baked 4x from it into `assets/images/platformer/emotes` by `node scripts/bake-emotes.mjs`) pop over the hero or an enemy and follow them (read from the frame array, so a squash never bends them). A speaker has one at a time. Rules (`emotesForEvent`, `emotesForAnswer`, tested): an enemy that makes contact shows "!" (the boss the red anger mark, and again when it roars in its introduction); a right answer makes the enemy dizzy (swirl) and the hero smile, the beating blow makes the hero cheer (stars, also at the boss's burst); a wrong answer makes the enemy laugh ("HA HA") and breaks the hero's heart; any lost heart shows the broken heart; a heart block a heart; a key an idea bulb; an opened lock a smile; a lock or a shut door without its key a "?"; a checkpoint a star; the hero smiles at the end of the ready pose; standing still for 7 s shows "zZ" every 4.5 s until they move; out of hearts shows the sad face. A balloon pops (0.2 to 1.18 to 1 in 340 ms about its tail), hangs with a small bob, then shrinks and fades (200 ms); reduced motion shows it still for its hold.

**Sounds** (`platformer-sfx-core.ts`, cues in `docs/SFX_SPEC.md`): every level has one ground (its terrain theme: grass, snow, sand, stone; the purple one is soil) and the hero's feet follow it: a quiet footstep each stride (`STRIDE`, every other footfall of the animation) on the ground, left then right (`footstepDue`), one Mario-style hop for every jump (`JUMP_CUE`), a landing sound by ground, and the thud of a defeated hero reaching the floor. A checkpoint has its own sound, and "Level complete!" is stamped with a firework (launch, then burst) over the jingle. The level warms only the players it needs (`warmSfxCues`), so the game's sounds do not push the UI's out of the player pool (now 40). Not used from the upload: the 60 s ambience loops (11 MB each, no looping in the effects runtime), wood, metal, ladder and dig sounds, bird flaps.

## 5e. Keeping it smooth

Every number that changes on a frame is a native view update, so the world is built to change as few as possible (rules, tested in `npm run test:platformer`):

- **One write per frame, read by everything.** The screen writes the per-frame array once; no React render happens per frame. Only `setSeconds` (once a second) and real events re-render the screen, and `PlatformerWorld` is a `memo` (its defaults are module constants, so an unchanged level state skips it), as are `Chunk`, `Coins`, `Gems`, `Blocks`, `Enemy`, `Water`, `Saw`, `Spring`, `MovingPlatform`, `SignPost`, `Checkpoint` and `Hero`.
- **Things that only drift for the eye are stepped.** A coin's bob, a gem's twinkle, a key, the unread sign's "!", a saw's turn and the water's flow read the clock through `stepped(t, fps)` (`world-fx-core.ts`: `DECOR_FPS` 20, `SPIN_FPS` 30, the shimmer 8), so their style is the same between steps and the UI thread sends nothing for them. Do not read `frame.value[1]` raw in a new decoration.
- **No loop per object.** Gems and blocks have no `ShineSweep` (each ran its own repeating clock, a layout state and a focus hook, which made levels with many gems or blocks stutter). A block's glint (`BlockGlint`) is one animated style off the world's time, constant between sweeps.
- **Out of sight is parked.** `fillFrame(..., viewPx)` writes `PARKED_PX` (a constant) for an enemy or a moving platform more than `CULL_MARGIN_TILES` beyond the view, instead of moving it every frame; coins, gems, blocks and saws only exist near the view (chunks), and the battle's partner is always drawn.
- **Chunks are cheap to come back to.** The pictures of a chunk are worked out once per level, theme and screen height (`chunkItems`), so scrolling back and forth costs mounts, not walks over tiles. Chunks keep one spare on each side so a mount happens off screen.

## 5d. The end of a level

There is no result card. Touching the open door starts the end on the map (`exit-core.ts`, one clock in ms; `ExitView.tsx`, `ResultPanel.tsx`): the camera scrolls to the door and the view zooms in on it (0 to 650), the door swings open (200) and the hero walks to its middle and steps in, shrinking away (600 to 1150), the door shuts behind them with a burst and a kick of the zoom (1200), the title is stamped at the top with the jingle ("Level complete!", 1450), the stars pop out over the door one by one (from 1800, 360 ms apart: the gold star with a burst for an earned star, the grey empty star for the others; both are `hud_star` and `hud_star_empty`, drawn in the pack's HUD style like the coin and heart: white rim, dark ink outline, flat fills, baked from `Vector/Tiles/hud_star*.svg`, which are built from the pack's own `star.svg`), and then the **result** fades in at the bottom (`ResultPanel`: no card, the text is written straight onto a dark gradient over the game; the numbers counted up, points to the next star, best time, lessons to review, and icon-only buttons: home, play again, next level), with the door still in view above it. Out of hearts is shorter: the hero falls to the floor if they were in the air (`landingY` in the core, gravity, the pit case stays put) and lies down (frame `down`, the sheet's last: on their back, head to the left, arms flopped out, crossed-out eyes; one still frame, no flash, no blinking, no looping), the view zooms in (or stays on the battle), "Out of hearts" is stamped and the panel rises. A level whose door is a boss's: the lock goes and the door pops when the boss's celebration unlocks it. Reduced motion: the panel is up at once with the stars at rest.

## 5c. Moments in the level

Timelines are pure maths in `world-fx-core.ts` (tested), drawn by `PlatformerWorld.tsx` and `WorldFx.tsx`. **Blocks:** a hit from below makes the block jump and squash (`blockBump`), a `?` or heart block that still has something in it glints now and then (`ShineSweep`), a coin or a heart springs out of it (`coinPop`, `heartPop`, with a burst for the heart), a brick breaks into four pieces that fly off, fall and fade (`brickPiece`), an opened lock bursts into stars. Wall torches flicker between the pack's two flame pictures (`Torch`, about six times a second, each torch a step apart). A picked-up coin jumps, swells and fades (`coinGet`). **Flags and the door:** a checkpoint flag is grey until the hero passes it, then it pops up, rocks and waves with a burst (`flagPop`); the goal door pops when it unlocks and when it swings open or shuts. **Signs:** the sign rocks when the hero steps up (`signRock`); a bobbing "!" over each unread sign asks to be read; the bubble (`SignBubble.tsx`: a sign icon and the words, typed out with the soft talking blip, `lesson.blip`, every third letter; the blips are cut off the moment the bubble closes or goes) pops out of the sign (`bubbleFrame`: scale 0.35 to 1 in 200 ms, ease-out), stays inside the screen, and shrinks back into it when the hero leaves. Reduced motion draws none of the one-shots.

**The boss's defeat** (`boss-victory-core.ts`, `BossVictory.tsx`; one clock in ms from when the battle panel closes, still on the battle's zoom): the beaten boss shudders (0 to 500, no flash), swells and squashes brighter (500 to 1000), and **bursts** at 1000 (gone at once, two rings of stars, a shake and a kick of the zoom, the explosion sound, a vibration) while the hero throws up their arms and confetti rains; the letterbox bars slide in and the bottom one stamps "<Boss> defeated!" (1250); the view zooms out (2300 to 2900) and the camera scrolls to the goal flag, which **unlocks** at 3600 (the lock goes, it pops up with a burst, "The door is open!"); the camera scrolls back to the hero and the bars leave (to 5400). The door stays shut (`flagLocked` follows the screen's `flagOpen`, not the core's `alive`) until that beat. Reduced motion skips all of it: the boss is gone and the door open at the blow.

## 5a. Starting a level

No start card. The screen opens on a **loading screen** (`LoadingScreen.tsx`: Shrodi idling, the level's name, the lessons' progress bar) while the level's pictures are preloaded (`preload.ts`, `levelSources` / `preloadSources` over `expo-asset`), its questions load and the world is mounted under the card (so the first frames are drawn already); it stays at least 0.7 s. Then it fades out and **Shrodi walks in** from just beyond the left edge to the start, **gets ready** (crouch, hop, landing, cheer, hold: `READY`, `readyPose` in `entrance-core.ts`, all from the hero sheet's own clips) and the **mission** drops in at the top (`MissionBanner.tsx`, text from `mission` in `story-levels.ts`) for 3.4 s as play and the HUD begin. The walk-in and ready run on the screen's clock while the world waits (water, coins and flag keep moving); reduced motion skips them. A restart (pause menu, Play again) goes straight into play.

## 5b. Story mode

Games > Atom Maze opens the level map (`app/explorer/story.tsx`): fifteen levels in three worlds (`story-levels.ts`, `STORY_LEVELS` and `STORY_WORLDS`: the five tutorial levels, then the five levels of Unit 1, one lesson a level, in an evergreen forest, then the five of Unit 2 in the desert; each later unit will add five more in a biome of its own), each opening when the one before it has been won (`story-core.ts`: `isLevelUnlocked`, `currentLevelIndex`; stars and best score in the user-state table `story_progress`, `services/story-progress.ts`, `hooks/use-story-progress.ts`). **The map** is a scrolling road of worlds: each world is a banner in its unit's colour (the lesson map's `unitPalette`: Unit N wears colour N, the tutorial green; its tag, title, stars earned and a window on its biome) over five stops strung along a winding dotted road (lit in the world's colour past a won stop), with the game's own scenery props along it (`decorFor`, by biome). The stops are the lesson map's own `LessonNode` pucks (a glyph each, fireflies and a progress ring round the next one), with their stars and name underneath; tapping one pops the lesson map's `NodeBubble` (reworded through its `copy` prop: `Level 7 · Meet the atom`, the level's blurb, Play / Play again, and a locked note), whose Play pushes `/explorer/platformer?level=<id>`. A world is dimmed until its first level is open; the map opens scrolled to the next level (`scrollTarget`). All the layout numbers are in `story-map-core.ts`. The old maze remains reachable only from code (`DifficultyPanel`), until it is removed.

**Levels** (`platformer-levels.ts`, format in `LevelDef`; every level also has a `scenery` layer and `signs`). The tutorial: 1 *Atom Meadow* is the tutorial (seven readable signs: run, jump, gaps, coins, battles, checkpoints, flag; nothing but ground); 2 *Proton Pines* (snow, trees) adds `?` coin blocks, heart blocks, bricks, planks and spikes; 3 *Neutron Dunes* (sand, desert) springs and a bee; 4 *Isotope Caverns* (stone, mushrooms) moving platforms, lifts, a key and a lock; 5 *Reactor Core* (purple, fading trees) uses all of it and ends with the Reactor Slime. Unit 1 (*Inside the Atom*, grass ground under the evergreen trees; each level asks the questions of one lesson): 6 *Matter Meadow* gems; 7 *Nucleus Nook* conveyor belts (with you, against you, toward spikes, across a pit); 8 *Element Hills* saws (on the ground, two at once, hanging overhead, in a rhythm); 9 *Isotope Isles* lava crossed by platforms, a spring and a lift, then a key and a lock; 10 *Stability Summit* all of it, with the Reactor Slime, its questions the unit's hardest. **A unit level adds at most ONE new thing** (`test:platformer` checks it), explained by its first sign near the start; the other two signs are a short lesson discussion (icon `lesson`): the first opens "Lesson N, <title>: ..." with what the lesson is about, the second adds one more idea. No tips. They teach around the level's questions and never state a fact those questions ask (the pool is the lesson's own bank items), so no sign is a giveaway. The bridge (`_`) and the gem block (`G`) open Unit 2 (levels 11 and 12). The new tiles: a gem (`$`, `gems`, `GEM_VALUE` 5 coins, event `gem`) and a gem block (`G`, bumped from below, reward `gem`); a saw (`Z`, `saws`, a hazard like spikes that can hang anywhere and turns on screen); a conveyor belt (`>` `<`, solid, `conveyor` +1 / -1, carries a hero who stands on it at `CONVEYOR_SPEED` 2.6 tiles a second, art is the pack's arrow block flipped for the left); a bridge (`_`, a one-way plank drawn as logs, `bridge`); lava (`%`, a pit like water with `water` = 2, drawn by the same wave sheet in orange). Mechanics in the core: `-` one-way plank (jump up through, stand on top), `?` coin block, `!` heart block, `=` brick (broken from below), `L` lock (opens with a key, `k`), `u` spring, `M` / `W` platforms that slide sideways or up and down (carrying the hero), `1`-`9` signs whose words are `signs[]` (a bubble shows while the hero stands near: event `sign`). On the map a sign is always the signpost tile; the picture of what it is about is in its speech bubble: an entry of `signs` is `{ text, icon }` (`SignIcon`; `SignIcon.tsx` draws it from the game's own sprites: the arrow buttons for "hold the arrows to run", the jump pad, a spring, a gem, a saw, lava, a flag, a door, a book for a lesson, the idea bulb for a tip...), a bare string gets the signpost in its bubble. Every story level's signs have a picture (tested). A level's ground runs on at least 3.4 tiles past its door (the exit zooms in on it; the Reactor Core has 9.5), tested. `platformer-view.ts` carries platforms and springs in the frame array (`platformSlot`, `springSlot`); `PlatformerWorld.tsx` draws the terrain by theme (`TERRAIN`, `tileSprite`), scenery, signs, blocks, keys, springs and platforms. `npm run test:platformer` checks that every level parses, has one start and flag, that ground steps are at most 2 tiles and gaps at most 4 unless a spring, plank or platform helps, and that level 1 can be walked by a bot.

**Fixed questions.** Each level has an ordered pool of bank item ids (easy to hard, from its unit); `assignQuestions` gives the enemies, from left to right, `hp + 2` ids each, so questions get harder along the level and the boss asks the last. `services/story-questions.ts` loads them (`contentRepository.getItemsByIds`; only `choice` and `true_or_myth` items fit, `story-questions-core.ts`), `story-battle-core.ts` serves them (an enemy asks its next one each fight and starts again when its list runs out; two options per battle, the right one plus one wrong). A wrong answer remembers the question's objective; when the level ends, `recommendLessons` turns the missed objectives into the lessons that teach them (`lessons.teaches`), and the result card lists "Review these lessons" (opening `/lesson/<id>`), with "Next level" after a win. The test `story: every fixed question exists in the content database` keeps the ids honest.

## 7. Sound

Files `game/platformer-{jump,coin,hurt,defeat,battle}.wav` (converted from the pack's `sfx_jump`, `sfx_coin`, `sfx_hurt`, `sfx_disappear`, `sfx_magic`; 16-bit mono 44.1 kHz, levelled with `scripts/normalize-sfx.mjs`), cues `game.platformer-*` (see `docs/SFX_SPEC.md`). A battle opens with `lesson.zoom-in` and closes with `lesson.zoom-out`; answers use the lessons' `lesson.answer-correct` and `lesson.answer-wrong`, a hit that does not beat the enemy `reward.pop` and a beaten enemy `reward.sparkle` (bright, never the old `game.platformer-defeat`, which sounded like a wrong answer after a right one), and a hurt `game.platformer-hurt`. The battle cue `game.platformer-battle` plays as the enemy is touched, the start `lesson.start`, the end `reward.finish-jingle` or `game.over`.

**Touch controls** (`Controls.tsx`, pictures `CONTROL_SPRITES` in `sprites.ts`): Kenney's Mobile Controls (CC0); only the used SVG sources are kept, in `assets/images/controls/vector` (with `License.txt`); the pieces used are baked 4x from those SVGs into `assets/images/controls/` (style A dark pads: `pad_left`, `pad_right`, `pad_circle`; the orange highlight outlines `*_lit`, drawn over a pad while it is pressed; `icon_arrow` and `icon_jump` glyphs). A pressed button sinks 3 dp and shrinks a little. The pictures are preloaded with the level's.
